Where does Tampa stand on poverty?

My adopted city, measured against nine other Florida cities across a decade of poverty data, on a single dashboard built to be read in under a minute.

Format
Annotated Tableau dashboard
Scope
10 Florida cities, 2012–2023
Project
“State of the Region”
Live
Tableau Public ↗

01 — The questionIs Tampa keeping up with the rest of Florida?

Regional leaders ask a simple question: are we getting better, and how do we compare? I took poverty rates for ten of Florida’s largest cities, from Jacksonville to Hialeah, and designed one dashboard that answers both halves at a glance. A decade-long trend sits on top and the latest ranking below, with Tampa highlighted throughout.

02 — What the data saysProgress, unevenly shared

  • Hialeah improved the most, with poverty down 7.4% since 2012.
  • Miami was the only city to get worse, up 0.4% over the same period.
  • Tampa improved gradually but still ranked third-highest in 2023, behind Tallahassee and Miami.
  • Port St. Lucie and Hialeah swung noticeably from year to year. Jacksonville ended the decade with one of the lowest rates of the group.
  • Tallahassee peaked at 31.1% in 2013 and stayed above 20% throughout. It is likely shaped by its large student population, which is worth testing before drawing conclusions.
Design choiceOne highlighted line, nine muted ones, and annotations written in plain sentences. The reader should get the story before they read an axis.

03 — What I’d do nextFrom “where” to “why”

The dashboard shows where each city stands. The next step is explaining it: join in population size, median income, housing costs and student population, then model which factors best predict a city’s poverty trajectory. That would let a regional planner see which levers actually separated Hialeah’s decline from Miami’s rise.
